Under the hood
Every formula this page uses

Nothing is hidden — the tool does four small computations, all from the paper's Section 7 machinery.

1 · Your weights

If your five percentages don't sum to 100, each is divided by the total so they do: weight = your % ÷ sum of all five. Everything downstream uses the normalized weights.

2 · Scenario per-share values (editable under "Advanced")

Each scenario's 2031 per-share value comes from the paper's valuation bridge (Figure 21): per-share = (2031 revenue × EV/S multiple) ÷ diluted shares, then the midpoint of the resulting range is used:

Why Superbear isn't zero: even a wind-down leaves cash and salable assets — the equity settles toward cash-adjusted option value, not nothing. Why diluted share counts differ by scenario: weaker execution means more equity issued at lower prices (the paper's Section 8 funding bridge).

3 · Expected value, multiple, and annualized return

E[2031] = sum of (weight × value) across the five scenarios. The multiple is E[2031] ÷ share price ($5.77 unless you change it), and the annualized figure solves multiple^(1/5.35) − 1 — 5.35 years from late August 2026 to year-end 2031. Deliberately not applied: a discount rate. Probability-weighting the Superbear branch already carries the risk adjustment; discounting on top would count risk twice. If you prefer discounted numbers, mentally shave ~2–3% a year off the annualized figure as a time-value haircut.

4 · The migration path (Figure 23)

The 2028 endpoint applies the paper's pre-committed de-risking rule to your starting vector: Superbear × 0.4 · Bear × 0.6 · Bull × 1.4 · Superbull × 1.4, with Middle absorbing whatever keeps the total at 100 (gains are scaled back if Middle would go negative). The three intermediate checkpoints are straight-line interpolations between your vector and that endpoint, and E[V] is recomputed at each one with the same formula as above. The rule reproduces the paper's own path exactly: 5/10/55/20/10 → 2/6/50/28/14. It is what "milestones keep landing" is assumed to be worth; a tripped wire instead jumps to the bear-shift vector (45/30/20/5/0).

5 · What this tool deliberately does not compute

Path probabilities between now and 2031 (a scenario reached early or late), correlation between your probabilities and the share counts, and any view on entry timing. The scenario economics themselves — revenue per truck, multiples, share counts — are the paper's illustrative, contestable assumptions; this page's job is to make the probability disagreement explicit, because that is where most arguments about AUR actually live.
